Our story

Why "Orbital"

Clemson CubeSat gave us the foundation: people who know how to design a small satellite, integrate it, and fly it.

A mission keeps going after the spacecraft leaves the rocket. There are ground passes to schedule, data to clean up, researchers waiting on results, and a new class of students who'll inherit the next build. We picked a name that covers all of that.

So we widened the scope. We still build satellites; we also plan larger missions, open our hardware to research, and speak up for aerospace in the Southeast, where plenty of good engineers currently leave to find this work.

Values

How we work

01

Build it to fly.

A design isn't done until it survives vibration, thermal cycling, and the people who have to operate it. We test early and write down what broke.

02

Write it down.

Reviews are open, documents stay current, and every lesson gets handed to the next team. If only one person understands a subsystem, it isn't finished.

03

Keep the South in the room.

Decisions about aerospace get made in a handful of places. We want students and companies from this region in those conversations, and we want to show the work can happen here.

Who can join

You don't need to know orbital mechanics yet.

Most members are undergraduates in engineering, computing, and the sciences. Plenty joined without any space experience and learned it next to someone a semester ahead of them.

We also work with faculty, grad students, local companies, and community groups. If you can help with a design review, a ground station, a school visit, or a funding conversation, we'd like to hear from you.

Contact our leadership

Got an experiment that needs orbit?

Or a classroom that wants a visit, or a company that wants to sponsor a build. Write to the team and we'll tell you honestly whether it fits.
